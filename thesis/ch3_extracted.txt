CHAPTER 3 RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY This section details the methodology employed in the development and evaluation of ForenXAI, an Explainable AI-driven framework with Retrieval-Augmented Generation for network log analysis. It describes the research design, the environment in which the study was conducted, the procedures for gathering and preparing data, the system architecture of the proposed tool, the tools and technologies used in its implementation, the step-by-step experimental procedures followed during development, and the evaluation metrics and validation techniques applied to assess the system's performance. 3.1 RESEARCH DESIGN General Guidelines: Discuss thoroughly the research methodology pursued by the proponents. A research methodology describes the techniques and procedures used to identify and analyze information regarding a specific research topic. It is a process by which researchers design their study so that they can achieve their objectives using the selected research instruments. There are three types of research methodology—quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method, which can be chosen based on the research objectives. (add GenAI , indicate, RAG based) This study follows a developmental research design, which is appropriate for studies whose primary objective is the creation, implementation, and evaluation of a functional technological artifact. Unlike purely descriptive or correlational research, developmental research involves the systematic process of designing, building, and testing a system against defined performance criteria. ForenXAI is the primary deliverable of this study, a working software tool rather than a theoretical model, and its success is measured by how well it performs against quantifiable evaluation metrics. The study is also applied in nature, as it directly addresses a practical problem identified in both the literature and practice: the inability of current network forensic tools to automatically analyze PCAP/PCAPng-based network logs and produce transparent, human-readable explanations of detected threats. Rather than contributing solely to theoretical knowledge, this study produces a tool that forensic investigators can directly utilize in their post-incident analysis workflows.

The CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset was developed through a collaborative project between the Communications Security Establishment (CSE) and the Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC). It contains 16,233,002 network-traffic instances collected over ten days in an AWS-based network environment and covers seven attack scenario s, including brute-force, Heartbleed, botnet, DoS, DDoS, web attacks, and network infiltration. The dataset also includes 80 traffic features extracted from captured network traffic using CICFlowMeter [ SHARAFALDIN, LASHKARI, GHORBANI] , [CANADIAN INSTI] . The TII-SSRC-23 dataset , introduced

The two datasets were merged because neither independently provides adequate coverage for the classification task. TII-SSRC-23 contains only 1,301 benign flows against 8,655,466 malicious records, a proportion insufficient for a classifier to learn a reliable representation of normal traffic, while CSE-CIC-IDS2018 supplies 13,390,249 benign flows. Conversely, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 contributes no reconnaissance class and no IoT botnet traffic, both of which TII-SSRC-23 provides. The merge is therefore complementary rather than merely additive.
